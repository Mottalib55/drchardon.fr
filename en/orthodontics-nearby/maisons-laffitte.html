Maisons-Laffitte and its residents

Maisons-Laffitte is a commune of approximately 24,000 inhabitants, widely known for its grand chateau designed by Francois Mansart and its deep connections to the world of horse racing. The town's hippodrome, one of the most important flat-racing tracks in France, and the extensive training grounds that border the Foret de Saint-Germain-en-Laye give Maisons-Laffitte a distinctive identity among the western suburbs of Paris. The equestrian heritage is visible everywhere -- in the wide, sandy training tracks that cross the town, in the stables and paddocks that adjoin residential streets, and in the lively atmosphere on race days.

Beyond the equestrian world, Maisons-Laffitte is an attractive residential town with a population that values quality of life. The town centre along the Avenue de Longueil features a mix of independent shops, restaurants, and services, while the residential streets -- particularly those near the park of the chateau -- are lined with handsome late-nineteenth and early-twentieth-century houses. The town has well-regarded schools, including the College Jean Cocteau and several primary schools, and the Lycee international de Saint-Germain-en-Laye is within easy reach for older students.

Orthodontic treatments for patients from Maisons-Laffitte

At her Colombes practice, she provides the full range of orthodontic treatments, selected on clinical grounds for each individual patient. For younger children from Maisons-Laffitte, interceptive orthodontics addresses problems during the mixed-dentition phase, typically between ages six and ten. Using age-appropriate appliances -- palatal expanders, functional appliances, or partial braces -- she can correct crossbites, guide jaw growth, create space for crowded permanent teeth, and address harmful habits such as prolonged thumb-sucking. This early work can substantially reduce the scope and duration of later comprehensive treatment.

Multi-bracket braces are the treatment she turns to most frequently for teenagers and for adults whose cases demand precise, three-dimensional control. Modern brackets -- whether metal or ceramic -- are smaller, more comfortable, and more efficient than their predecessors. Combined with a carefully planned sequence of archwires and, where needed, auxiliary devices such as elastic bands, they allow her to manage complex corrections involving crowding, rotations, deep bites, and skeletal discrepancies. she discusses the choice between metal and ceramic brackets with each patient, explaining the pros and cons of each.

Clear aligners are particularly popular among adult patients from Maisons-Laffitte. These custom-made transparent trays fit snugly over the teeth and are changed every one to two weeks, progressively moving the teeth towards their planned positions. Adults appreciate the discretion and the ability to remove the trays for meals, social events, and oral hygiene. she designs each aligner case digitally, validating every planned tooth movement before production begins. Not every case is suited to aligners, and she is always upfront about that. When brackets would produce a better or more predictable result, she recommends them.

Every treatment concludes with a structured retention phase: bonded wires, removable retainers, and scheduled follow-up visits. Retention is not optional -- it is how the investment in treatment is protected over time.

Treatment process for a patient from Maisons-Laffitte

If you are in Maisons-Laffitte and considering orthodontic treatment, the process begins with a telephone call or online enquiry to book your first consultation. At this initial appointment, she carries out a complete clinical examination and take a set of digital records: an intraoral 3D scan, a panoramic X-ray, a cephalometric X-ray, and standardised photographs. The 3D scan replaces traditional impressions -- there is no paste, no discomfort, and the result is more accurate.

she thens analyse these records thoroughly, assessing tooth positions, skeletal relationships, and soft-tissue balance. From this analysis, she creates a personalised treatment plan that addresses all aspects of the malocclusion. At a follow-up appointment, she presents this plan to you with full transparency: what the problems are, what she proposes to do, which appliance she recommends and why, how long treatment is expected to take, and what the total cost will be. she alwayses explain alternatives where they exist, and she encourages you to take time to consider the plan before committing.

Once treatment is underway, you visit the practice every 6 to 12 weeks. Each appointment is focused and efficient -- she checks progress, make adjustments, deliver new aligner trays if applicable, and discuss any questions or concerns. Between appointments, she provides clear instructions for home care and she is available by phone or email if something unexpected comes up.

At the end of active treatment, the appliance is removed and retention devices are fitted. she schedules follow-up visits at increasing intervals over the subsequent two years to ensure stability. The entire treatment journey -- from first records to the last retention check -- is managed by me at the Colombes practice, ensuring complete continuity of care.
