The 14th arrondissement and its residents

The 14th arrondissement possesses a strong identity, forged by decades of artistic and intellectual life. The Montparnasse quarter was, in the 1920s, the centre of global creativity: painters, writers and musicians gathered in the cafes along the boulevard. Today, this tradition continues through the Fondation Cartier, the galleries on the Rue Campagne-Première and the art-house cinemas scattered throughout the arrondissement. The Cimetiere du Montparnasse, where Baudélaire, Sartre, Beauvoir, Gainsbourg and many others rest, remains one of the most visited sites on the Left Bank.

But the 14th is also and above all a residential arrondissement. The Parc Montsouris, a vast 15-hectare green space désignéd in the 19th century, is the lung of the southern part of the arrondissement. Families gather there at weekends, children play, runners jog the paths. Around the park, streets lined with Haussmann buildings and small town houses offer a peaceful and sought-after living environment. The Cite internationale universitaire de Paris, with its architectural pavilions représenting dozens of countries, adds a unique cosmopolitan dimension to this neighbourhood.

The Catacombes de Paris, whose entrance is at the Place Denfert-Rochereau, attract hundreds of thousands of visitors each year to these underground galleries where the bones of millions of Parisians rest. The Lion of Belfort, which stands at the centre of the square, is a visual landmark known to every resident of the 14th. Place Denfert-Rochereau is also the main transport hub of the arrondissement, at the intersection of metro 4, metro 6 and RER B.

Demographically, the 14th is home to a diverse population: young professionals attracted by rents that are somewhat more affordable than in the central arrondissements, families who have been settled for many years in the residential neighbourhoods, students from the Cite universitaire and nearby faculties, and retirees attached to their neighbourhood. Treatment process for a patient from the 14th

The care pathway begins with a thorough initial consultation. She carries out a complète clinical examination, takes intraoral and extraoral photographs, panoramic X-rays and, if necessary, lateral cephalometric radiographs. Digital impressions are taken directly at the practice using a latest-generation intraoral scanner, avoiding the traditional alginate impressions that are often unpleasant. All these data enable her to establish a precise diagnosis and design a tailor-made treatment plan.

At a second appointment, she présents the treatment plan in détail. She explains the different options available, the expected timeline, the requirements of each technique and the total cost. Her approach is founded on transparency: you know all the fees before making your décision, with no surprises or hidden costs. She provides a détailed estimate that you can submit to your supplementary health insurance to assess their coverâge.

During the active treatment phase, follow-up appointments are spaced 6 to 12 weeks apart. For a patient from the 14th, this means a trip to Colombes approximately once every month and a half to three months. Each check-up allows her to verify the progress of tooth movements, adjust the appliance if necessary and answer your questions. Appointments are scheduled in advance so that you can organise your diary without constraints. She remains available between consultations for any emergency or question, by phone or email.

The retention phase is the final step, as important as the active treatment itself. She places a bonded retainer wire on the inner surface of the teeth and/or prescribes a night retainer tray to keep the teeth in their corrected position. Without retention, teeth have a natural tendency to drift back towards their original position, which would compromise the results achieved. Regular checks during the first months following appliance removal ensure the long-term stability of the outcome.
