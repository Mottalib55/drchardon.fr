How to reach the practice from Aubervilliers

By car: The journey from Aubervilliers to Colombes takes approximately fifteen minutes outside rush hours. From central Aubervilliers, the most direct route joins the Boulevard Peripherique via the Porte d'Aubervilliers or the Porte de la Villette, then heads towards Porte de Clichy or Porte de Champerret to reach the A86 towards Colombes. Another option is to go via the departmental road through Saint-Denis and Gennevilliers before reaching Colombes from the north. Parking around the practice is accessible, with spaces available in the residential streets near the Gare du Stade. During rush hours, particularly between 8:00 and 9:30 in the morning and between 5:30 and 7:30 in the evening, the journey may be ten to fifteen minutes longer depending on traffic on the Peripherique.

By public transport: Several routes connect Aubervilliers to Colombes in approximately thirty minutes. From the Aubervilliers-Pantin-Quatre Chemins station on metro line 7, travel to La Defense via a change at Opera on RER A line 1, then take Transilien L from La Defense to the Gare du Stade. Another route uses metro line 12 from Front Populaire or Aime Cesaire station, alight at Saint-Lazare, then take Transilien L towards Colombes. Line 12, whose recent extension serves northern Aubervilliers, greatly facilitates the connection to western Paris. Future metro extensions, including Line 15 of the Grand Paris Express serving Aubervilliers, will further improve connections in the coming years.

For cyclists, the Aubervilliers-Colombes journey is achievable in approximately thirty-five to forty minutes. The route partly follows the Canal Saint-Denis, then crosses Saint-Denis and Gennevilliers before reaching Colombes. Cycling infrastructure is improving along this route, and users of electric bicycles or long-term Velib' subscriptions can significantly reduce this travel time.

Orthodontic treatments for patients from Aubervilliers

Clear aligners have become the treatment of choice for many adult patients from Aubervilliers. These transparent, removable trays offer a discreet way to straighten teeth without the visual impact of traditional braces. Dr Chardon designs each treatment digitally using 3D intraoral scans, which allows her to show patients a simulation of the expected result before treatment begins. Aligners are changed every one to two weeks at home, and office visits are scheduled every 6 to 12 weeks to monitor progress.

Multi-bracket braces are available in both metal and ceramic versions. Metal braces remain the most reliable and versatile option for complex cases, including those involving significant bite correction or jaw discrepancy. Ceramic brackets are tooth-coloured and offer a more aesthetic alternative while maintaining the mechanical efficiency of fixed appliances. For many teenagers from Aubervilliers, braces represent the most effective treatment pathway.

Interceptive orthodontics targets children aged 6 to 11, during the transitional period between primary and permanent teeth. At this stage, certain problems such as posterior crossbites, narrow palates, or severe crowding can be addressed with short-term treatments that simplify or even prevent the need for comprehensive braces later on. Dr Chardon recommends an initial orthodontic evaluation at age 7 for all children from Aubervilliers, in line with professional recommendations.

Adult orthodontics is a significant and growing segment of her practice. Adults from Aubervilliers consult her for a variety of reasons: teeth that were never properly aligned, relapse after earlier treatment, or functional issues such as difficulty biting or jaw pain. Modern techniques mean that treatment is comfortable, discreet, and achievable at any age.

Treatment process for a patient from Aubervilliers

Beginning orthodontic treatment at the practice follows a structured and transparent process. The first step is to schedule an initial consultation, which can be done by phone or via the website. This appointment lasts approximately 45 minutes and includes a complete clinical examination, digital photographs, X-rays, and, if needed, a 3D scan of the teeth using an intraoral scanner. This modern scanning technology eliminates the need for traditional impressions and provides extremely accurate digital models of the dental arches.

Based on these records, Dr Chardon develops a personalised treatment plan. At a follow-up appointment, she presents this plan in detail, explaining the recommended approach, the alternatives considered, the expected timeline, and the total cost. She believes in complete transparency: there are no hidden fees or unexpected charges. She also reviews the coverage provided by health insurance and offers payment plans to spread the cost over the duration of treatment if needed.

Once the treatment plan is agreed upon, active treatment begins. For fixed braces, this involves bonding the brackets and placing the initial archwire. For aligners, she takes a final scan if necessary and orders the custom trays, which are ready within a few weeks. From that point, patients visit the practice every 6 to 12 weeks for adjustments or progress checks.

When active treatment is complete, Dr Chardon places retainers to stabilise the result. She uses bonded wire retainers and, when appropriate, removable retainer trays. Retention is not optional; it is an essential part of treatment, and she follows up over the ensuing months to make sure everything stays in place.
