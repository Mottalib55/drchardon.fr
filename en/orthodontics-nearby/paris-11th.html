The 11th arrondissement and its residents

The 11th arrondissement is the most densely populated arrondissement in Paris. With approximately 150,000 inhabitants concentrated in 3.67 km2, it embodies the intensity of Parisian life at its peak. It is a district deeply rooted in the capital's popular history: the great insurrections of the 19th century, the Paris Commune and the workers' movement all left their mark on the streets of Charonne, the Faubourg-Saint-Antoine and La Roquette. This historical memory now blends with a vibrant modernity that makes the 11th one of the most sought-after arrondissements among young professionals and families.

The Bastille area, at the border with the 4th and 12th arrondissements, remains a major landmark. The Place de la Bastille, with its opera house and its pleasure port, is a crossroads of urban life where walkers, cyclists and night owls intersect. The Boulevard Richard-Lenoir, with its twice-weekly market, is a Sunday meeting point for thousands of families. Further north, the Oberkampf area has undergone a remarkable transformation since the 2000s, evolving from a discreet artisan quarter to one of the Parisian hotspots for nightlife and dining. The Rue Oberkampf, the Rue Jean-Pierre Timbaud and the Rue de la Fontaine-au-Roi concentrate an impressive density of bars, restaurants and concert venues.

The Charonne neighbourhood, in the eastern part of the 11th, has preserved a more village-like atmosphere. The cobbled streets around the Eglise Saint-Germain de Charonne, the Passage de la Main d'Or and the Cour Damoye near Bastille: these preserved spots bear witness to an older Paris that resists standardisation. The Rue de la Roquette, the central artery of the 11th, connects Bastille to the Place Leon Blum and concentrates an eclectic mix of shops, galleries and restaurants.

The Place de la Republique, shared with the 3rd and 10th arrondissements, is another major hub. Redesigned as a vast pedestrian esplanade, it has become a place for civic gatherings and daily life. The 11th is served by an exceptional metro network: lines 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 and 9 cross the arrondissement, making it one of the best-connected districts in Paris. This excellent service greatly facilitates travel to the western suburbs and, by extension, to Colombes.

The population of the 11th is very young and dynamic. The median age is among the lowest in Paris, with a strong proportion of 25 to 40-year-olds. Creative professions, freelancers, startups and digital careers are overrepresented. At the same time, the 11th remains a socially diverse arrondissement, with social housing and a mix that constitutes its richness. Families with children are numerous, attracted by the neighbourhood life, the many schools and green spaces like the Square Maurice Gardette or the Jardin Truillot. This diversity is reflected in her patient base from the 11th: she treats young adults seeking discreet alignment as well as parents bringing their children for a first orthodontic assessment.

Orthodontic treatments offered to patients from the 11th

Dr Chardon's practice is exclusively dedicated to orthodontics, and she offers the full range of current techniques to meet each patient's needs. Clear aligners are the most requested treatment by patients from the 11th arrondissement. This system relies on a series of custom-made transparent trays that gradually shift the teeth towards their ideal position. The trays are removable, allowing normal eating, drinking and brushing. They are virtually invisible when worn, making them perfectly suited to the young professionals of the 11th who wish to correct their smile without anyone noticing. She personally supervises each stage of treatment, from initial digital planning to in-progress adjustments.

Multi-bracket braces remain the reference technique for complex orthodontic cases. The brackets, whether metal or ceramic, are bonded to the outer surface of the teeth and connected by an archwire that applies the necessary forces for tooth movement. Ceramic brackets, in a tooth-coloured shade, offer an appreciable aesthetic compromise. This technique is particularly effective in teenagers, whose bone growth facilitates tooth movement. She offers teenagers from the 11th a tailored approach, with appointments compatible with school schedules.

Interceptive orthodontics is aimed at children from the age of 6-7. An early orthodontic assessment can detect jaw development abnormalities, harmful habits such as thumb-sucking or mouth breathing, or emerging dental crowding. A short interceptive treatment, generally 6 to 12 months, can correct these problems before they worsen and significantly reduce the need for treatment during adolescence. She strongly recommends that parents in the 11th have their children examined at this age, even if no problem is apparent. Prevention is always preferable to curative treatment.

For adults, she treats a wide range of situations: dental crowding, open bite, deep bite, crossbite and interdental spaces. Some adults consult for aesthetic reasons, others for functional reasons such as joint pain linked to a malocclusion. In all cases, she establishes a comprehensive treatment plan that takes into account the periodontal status, any existing restorations and the patient's goals. Adult treatment duration typically ranges from 12 to 24 months, depending on the complexity of the case.

Treatment process for a patient from the 11th

The journey begins with a thorough initial consultation. Dr Chardon dedicates approximately one hour to this first appointment to take the time to listen, examine the mouth, take the necessary intraoral and extraoral photographs and the X-rays essential for diagnosis. If you are coming by public transport from the 11th, she suggests taking the Transilien L from Saint-Lazare and alighting at the Gare du Stade: the practice is just a few minutes' walk away. The initial consultation is the opportunity to establish a comprehensive diagnosis and to discuss treatment options together. She presents the different possibilities, their advantages, their limitations, the expected timelines and the costs. You leave with all the information needed to make your decision with full knowledge of the facts.

Once treatment has begun, follow-up appointments are scheduled every 6 to 12 weeks, depending on the technique used and the progress of treatment. These appointments are short, generally 20 to 30 minutes, and organised to minimise your waiting time. She checks the progression of treatment, makes the necessary adjustments (archwire changes, attachment placement, new tray handover) and verifies that oral hygiene is well maintained. Between appointments, she remains reachable by phone or email for any question or emergency. If a bracket comes off or a tray causes a problem, she sees patients as quickly as possible to resolve the issue without delaying treatment.

The retention phase is a step she takes very seriously, as it guarantees the long-term stability of results. At the end of active treatment, she places a bonded retainer wire on the inner surface of the teeth, invisible from the outside, to maintain the alignment achieved. For cases that require it, she also prescribes a night retainer tray. Regular checks, at least during the year following appliance removal, ensure that the results are lasting. A well-finished treatment means a smile that stays aligned over time.
