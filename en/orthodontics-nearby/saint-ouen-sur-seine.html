Saint-Ouen-sur-Seine and its residents

With a population of approximately 52,000, Saint-Ouen has attracted major urban renewal projects, including the development of the Docks de Saint-Ouen district along the Seine, which has brought new housing, offices, parks, and cultural spaces to the area.

The arrival of metro line 14 at the Mairie de Saint-Ouen station has further boosted the town's appeal, connecting it directly to central Paris and to the Saint-Denis Pleyel hub. The neighbourhoods of Vieux Saint-Ouen, Victor Hugo, and Debain-Michelet each have their own character, ranging from the lively streets around the Rue des Rosiers to the quieter residential blocks along the Boulevard Jean-Jaures.

The population includes a significant number of young professionals and families with children, attracted by the relatively more affordable property prices compared to neighbouring Clichy or the 17th and 18th arrondissements of Paris. Orthodontic treatments for patients from Saint-Ouen-sur-Seine

Her practice offers every major category of orthodontic treatment, and she adapts her recommendations to each patient's clinical needs and personal preferences. Here is what residents of Saint-Ouen-sur-Seine can expect.

Interceptive orthodontics. she recommends that children have their first orthodontic assessment around age 6 or 7, when the first permanent molars and incisors are erupting. At this stage, problems like posterior crossbites, anterior open bites, and severe crowding can be identified and addressed with targeted early intervention. For families in Saint-Ouen, catching these issues before they worsen can simplify later treatment and, in some cases, eliminate the need for braces altogether.

Braces for teenagers. Multi-bracket braces, whether metal or ceramic, remain the gold standard for treating complex malocclusions in adolescents. she uses low-friction self-ligating systems that deliver consistent, gentle forces, often reducing overall treatment time and the number of adjustment visits required. Treatment duration typically ranges from 12 to 24 months, with follow-up appointments scheduled every 6 to 12 weeks at the practice.

Clear aligners. For patients who prefer a more discreet approach, clear aligner therapy is an excellent option. Custom-made transparent trays are worn sequentially, each one moving the teeth a fraction of a millimetre closer to their final position. Aligners are removable, making it easy to maintain good oral hygiene and eat without restriction. she finds that many of her adult patients from Saint-Ouen gravitate toward this option, though it is also suitable for many teenagers.

Surgical orthodontics. In cases where the jaw bones themselves are significantly misaligned, orthodontic treatment may need to be combined with corrective jaw surgery. she works closely with oral and maxillofacial surgeons to plan and execute these combined treatments, handling the orthodontic component before and after the surgical procedure. While these cases are less common, having the expertise to manage them is an important part of comprehensive orthodontic practice.

Treatment process for a patient from Saint-Ouen-sur-Seine

Starting orthodontic treatment at her practice follows a clear, structured process that she explains fully from the outset.

Your first visit is a comprehensive consultation lasting about 45 minutes. she examines your teeth, gums, and jaw joints, take digital scans and photographs, and order any necessary radiographs. We discuss your concerns and what you hope to achieve, and she gives you an initial impression of what treatment might involve.

she thens spend time analysing your records in detail and preparing a customised treatment plan. This includes a clear explanation of the recommended approach, the expected duration, the total cost, and the payment options available, including staged payment plans. she sends the plan to you ahead of your second appointment so you can review it at home.

If you choose to proceed, we schedule the placement appointment. For fixed braces, this involves bonding brackets to your teeth and inserting the first archwire. For aligner treatment, you receive your initial sets of trays along with detailed instructions on wear and care. From this point, you visit the practice every 6 to 12 weeks for adjustments, progress checks, and new aligner trays as needed.

When active treatment is complete, we move to the retention phase. she bonds a thin wire behind your front teeth and, where appropriate, provide a removable retainer for night-time use. This phase is essential for maintaining your results long-term. she continues to see you periodically during retention to ensure everything stays in place.
