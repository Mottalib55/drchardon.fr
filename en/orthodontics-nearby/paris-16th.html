The 16th arrondissement and its residents

The 16th arrondissement is the largest arrondissement in Paris by area and one of the most distinctive. With a population of approximately 165,000 inhabitants, it stretches from the Arc de Triomphe in the north to the Porte d'Auteuil in the south, encompassing some of the most prestigious residential addresses in the capital. The district is defined by its wide avenues, elegant Haussmann-era and Art Deco buildings, and abundant green space: the Bois de Boulogne runs along its entire western edge, and within the arrondissement itself you find the Jardins du Trocadero, the Jardins du Ranelagh, and the Jardin des Serres d'Auteuil.

The 16th is often described as one of the wealthiest and most conservative quarters of Paris, but it is also remarkably diverse in its own way. The Passy and Muette quarters have a village-like character, with independent shops, local markets, and a strong community feel. The Auteuil neighbourhood in the south is quieter and more residential, home to many families with school-age children. The northern section, closer to the Etoile and the Trocadero, is more international, with a concentration of diplomatic residences, international schools, and expatriate families.

This international dimension is important. The 16th is home to numerous English-speaking families, many associated with embassies, international organisations, or multinational companies. Schools such as the International School of Paris and various bilingual institutions serve this community. she regularlies treat patients from these families and am comfortable conducting consultations entirely in English. she understands the concerns specific to expatriate families, including the possibility that treatment may need to be continued by a colleague in another country if a move occurs during the course of care.

Families in the 16th tend to place a high value on quality healthcare and are often well-informed about treatment options. Many arrive at consultations having done considerable research, and she appreciates that -- it makes for productive conversations about what each treatment can realistically achieve.

Orthodontic treatments available

she offers the full spectrum of orthodontic treatments at her Colombes practice. Each case begins with a thorough diagnostic assessment, and she recommends a technique only after carefully analysing the patient's specific situation.

Clear aligners are highly sought after by patients from the 16th arrondissement, both adults and teenagers. These custom-fabricated transparent trays are worn over the teeth and changed at prescribed intervals to achieve progressive tooth movement. Their near-invisibility is a major advantage for patients in social or professional settings where aesthetics matter. Aligners are also removable, which simplifies eating and oral hygiene. she works with advanced aligner systems that allow her to plan treatment digitally and show patients a simulation of their expected results before treatment begins.

Multi-bracket braces provide the highest degree of precision and control for complex orthodontic corrections. Cases involving severe crowding, significant overbites or underbites, impacted teeth, or skeletal discrepancies often require the mechanical advantages that only fixed braces can provide. she uses both metal and ceramic brackets. Ceramic brackets are tooth-coloured and significantly less visible, which appeals to many adult patients from the 16th who need braces but want a more refined appearance during treatment.

Interceptive orthodontics is aimed at children typically between 6 and 10 years old. At this stage, she can identify developing problems and intervene while the child's jaw is still growing. Common early interventions include palatal expansion to address narrow upper jaws, correction of anterior crossbites, and management of harmful oral habits. Not every child needs early treatment -- sometimes the best course of action is to monitor and wait. she alwayses explain her reasoning clearly to parents.

Teenage orthodontics addresses the comprehensive correction of the bite once most permanent teeth have erupted, typically between ages 11 and 16. This is the classic period for full orthodontic treatment, and the choice between braces and aligners depends on the complexity of the case and the teenager's preferences and responsibility level.

Adult orthodontics has become a significant part of her practice. Adults from the 16th often come to her seeking to correct long-standing misalignments, improve their bite function, or prepare their teeth for restorative work recommended by their dentist. Orthodontic treatment in adulthood is entirely feasible and can produce excellent results, provided the teeth and bone are healthy.

Treatment process

Her treatment process is methodical and transparent. It begins with an initial consultation, during which she performs a comprehensive clinical examination. she assesses the alignment of the teeth, the relationship between the upper and lower jaws, the facial profile, and any functional issues such as difficulty chewing or jaw discomfort. she alsos listen carefully to the patient's own concerns and expectations, because these shape the treatment plan as much as the clinical findings do.

The next step is the collection of diagnostic records: digital impressions, panoramic and cephalometric radiographs, and intraoral and extraoral photographs. These records are analysed in detail to produce a personalised treatment plan. At a dedicated planning appointment, she presents the plan to the patient (or to the patient and their parents, in the case of children and teenagers). she explains the proposed approach, the alternatives, the expected timeline, and the costs, including what social security and mutuelles typically cover. she encourages questions and never rush this conversation.

Treatment appointments are scheduled every 6 to 12 weeks, depending on the type of appliance and the phase of treatment. Each visit is focused and efficient, usually lasting 20 to 30 minutes. she updates the patient on progress at every appointment and adjust the treatment plan if necessary. From the 16th arrondissement, these regular visits are easy to integrate into a busy calendar thanks to the straightforward transport connections to Colombes.

After the active treatment phase, retention is critical. she bonds fixed retainers to the inner surfaces of the front teeth and supply removable retainer trays for nighttime wear. she thens schedule periodic follow-up appointments to verify that the teeth remain stable in their new positions. Good retention habits are the key to lasting results, and she makes sure every patient understands their importance.
