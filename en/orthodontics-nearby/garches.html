How to reach the practice from Garches

By car, the journey from Garches to the Colombes practice takes approximately 15 minutes under normal traffic conditions. The most direct route follows the D907, then the N13 towards La Defense, before joining the A86 northbound towards Colombes. Parking around the practice is easy, with street spaces and a car park within a short walk. An alternative route passes through Vaucresson and Nanterre along the boulevards.

By public transport, the journey from Garches takes approximately 30 minutes. The Garches-Marnes-la-Coquette station, served by the Transilien L, connects directly to La Defense Grande Arche in around ten minutes. From La Defense, a connection on the Transilien L towards Colombes reaches the Gare du Stade. The frequency of trains on the L line is high during peak hours, facilitating appointment scheduling.

For cyclists, the roughly 12-kilometre distance between Garches and Colombes can be covered in 35 to 40 minutes. The route follows the cycle paths along the Seine via Suresnes and Puteaux, a pleasant and safe ride along the riverbanks. Many residents of Garches work in the La Defense business district or in Nanterre. The Colombes practice sits north of this economic axis, allowing patients to combine an orthodontic appointment with their work commute.

Garches and its residents

Garches is a commune of approximately 18,500 inhabitants, situated on the southern slope of the hillside overlooking the Seine, between Saint-Cloud and Vaucresson. It is a predominantly residential town, remarkably green, offering a privileged living environment just a few kilometres from Paris. The built heritage consists largely of bourgeois villas dating from the early 20th century, interspersed with some recent upmarket residences. The quiet, tree-lined streets and the immediate proximity of the Saint-Cloud national forest give Garches an almost rural atmosphere, rare in the Hauts-de-Seine.

The Raymond-Poincare Hospital is the institutional heart of the commune. A national reference centre for motor disability, neurological rehabilitation and rare diseases, it attracts healthcare professionals, researchers and nursing staff who often choose to live in Garches or neighbouring communes. This concentration of medical personnel influences the sociological composition of the town and the expectations regarding care: residents of Garches are informed, demanding and committed to quality treatment.

The Haras de Jardy, located on the edge of Garches and Marnes-la-Coquette, is an emblematic site. This renowned equestrian centre hosts competitions and training. Families in Garches also benefit from numerous sports and cultural facilities, from the media library to local associations to the parks and gardens throughout the urban fabric.

The population of Garches is characterised by a significant proportion of long-established families, retirees attached to their living environment, and young households attracted by the quality of schools and the tranquillity of the surroundings. All these categories are reflected in the profile of patients Dr Chardon sees at the Colombes practice.

Orthodontic treatments for patients from Garches

Clear aligners are the most popular solution among adult patients from Garches. These transparent, removable trays correct malocclusions with great discretion. Treatment is digitally planned: from 3D impressions taken by the intraoral scanner, a movement protocol is developed tray by tray. This approach particularly appeals to healthcare professionals at the Raymond-Poincare Hospital, who appreciate having a specialist practice nearby. The removable nature of the aligners also facilitates mask-wearing in a hospital environment.

Multi-bracket braces remain the reference technique for complex cases in both teenagers and adults. Metal or ceramic brackets, combined with the latest-generation archwires, treat the full range of malocclusions: severe crowding, open bites, deep overbites, crossbites, diastemas. Comfort has improved considerably in recent years. For teenagers from Garches, multi-bracket treatment is often the most effective and reliable choice.

Interceptive orthodontics is for children aged 6 to 9 in whom a growth discrepancy, crossbite, mouth breathing or lack of space for permanent teeth is detected early. Intervening at this stage takes advantage of residual growth to guide arch development. Families in Garches consult willingly for a first orthodontic assessment around age 7, in line with professional recommendations.

Adult orthodontics accounts for a growing share of her consultations. Motivations vary: correcting long-standing crowding, aligning teeth before prosthetic treatment, or improving smile aesthetics. She takes the time to explain the options, durations and limitations of each technique, so that every patient can make an informed decision.

Treatment process for a patient from Garches

The first consultation lasts approximately 45 minutes. This is an essential period for a thorough clinical examination, intra-oral and extra-oral photographs, digital scanner impressions and the necessary radiographs. This complete assessment allows Dr Chardon to establish a precise diagnosis and understand the situation in its entirety. The patient leaves with a clear understanding of the findings and possible solutions.

The treatment plan is presented at a second appointment. She outlines the objectives, the recommended technique, estimated duration, follow-up appointment schedule and detailed cost. A full quote is provided with information on French social security coverage (for patients under 16) and supplementary health insurance. There is no pressure and no artificial urgency -- her role is to inform accurately so the patient can make the best decision.

Active treatment involves check-up appointments every 6 to 12 weeks. For a patient from Garches, the 15-minute drive makes these visits easy to fit into a busy schedule. In the event of discomfort or any incident between appointments, the practice responds promptly.

The retention phase begins immediately upon removal of the active appliance. A bonded retainer wire is placed on the inner surface of the incisors, supplemented by a night-time retainer tray. Retention check-ups are scheduled at regular intervals during the first two years, then less frequently. This rigour in post-treatment follow-up ensures lasting results.
