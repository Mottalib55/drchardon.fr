La Courneuve and its residents

La Courneuve is a commune of approximately 45,000 residents, situated in the heart of the Seine-Saint-Denis department. It is perhaps best known for two things: the Parc Georges-Valbon (formerly Parc Departemental de La Courneuve), one of the largest urban parks in the Ile-de-France region at over 400 hectares, and the annual Fete de l'Humanite, which for decades drew hundreds of thousands of visitors to the park each September.

Beyond these landmarks, La Courneuve is a working town with a complex urban fabric. The 4000 estate in the south, one of the most recognisable grands ensembles in France, has undergone extensive demolition and reconstruction as part of the national urban renewal programme (ANRU). The centre-ville around the Mairie and the Place du 8 Mai 1945 is the commercial heart, while the northern neighbourhoods near the Route de Flandre are more industrial in character. New residential developments are gradually changing the town's profile, attracting younger residents and families.

The youthful demographics of La Courneuve generate strong demand for children's and teenagers' orthodontic care. The town's schools and colleges refer a steady stream of young patients to orthodontic practitioners in the wider area, and she sees many of them at her Colombes practice. At the same time, adults in La Courneuve are increasingly aware of the benefits of orthodontic treatment and are choosing to invest in their dental health and appearance later in life.

Orthodontic treatments for patients from La Courneuve

she offers a comprehensive range of orthodontic treatments at her Colombes practice, and every plan she creates is individually designed. For patients from La Courneuve, here is a summary of what is available.

Interceptive orthodontics for children. A first visit around age 6 or 7 lets me identify problems early. Crossbites, protruding upper incisors, open bites, and crowding can all be addressed with targeted appliances while the child is still growing. Early treatment in some cases can eliminate the need for braces during adolescence entirely, and even when full treatment is still needed later, the early phase typically makes it shorter and simpler. For families in La Courneuve, this approach represents a smart investment in their child's future dental health.

Multi-bracket braces. For teenagers and some adults, fixed braces remain the most powerful tool for correcting moderate to severe malocclusions. she uses self-ligating metal and ceramic brackets that combine efficiency with improved patient comfort. The low-friction design allows teeth to move more freely, which often translates to fewer adjustment visits and shorter overall treatment time. A typical course lasts 12 to 24 months, with visits to the practice every 6 to 12 weeks for wire changes and progress checks.

Clear aligner therapy. If discretion is a priority, clear aligners offer an alternative that is nearly invisible. Each set of custom-made transparent trays is engineered using digital planning software, and she supervises the entire process from start to finish. Aligners work well for mild to moderate crowding, spacing, and certain bite issues. Patients from La Courneuve who choose aligners often tell her they appreciate being able to remove the trays for important meetings, social events, or meals.

Adult orthodontics. Whether you are 25 or 55, it is not too late for orthodontic treatment. Many adults from La Courneuve come to her having lived with crooked teeth, gaps, or an uncomfortable bite for decades. Modern techniques, including lingual braces placed behind the teeth and clear aligners, allow us to treat these issues discreetly. she alsos coordinate with your general dentist when orthodontic treatment needs to precede restorative work such as crowns, bridges, or implants, ensuring all aspects of your dental care fit together seamlessly.

Treatment process for a patient from La Courneuve

If you are a resident of La Courneuve considering orthodontic treatment, here is what the process looks like at her practice.

It all begins with a thorough initial consultation. During this appointment, which typically lasts 45 minutes, she examines your teeth, bite, and jaw joints. she captures a digital scan of your dental arches, take clinical photographs from multiple angles, and order any radiographs necessary for a complete picture. Most importantly, we talk. she wants to understand what brought you here, what bothers you about your teeth, and what outcome you are hoping for.

After the consultation, she conducts a comprehensive analysis of your records. she studies tooth positions, measure jaw relationships, assess root health and bone support, and develop a detailed mechanical plan for how to move your teeth safely and efficiently. The result is a personalised treatment proposal that she presents at your second visit. It covers the recommended approach, the timeline, a full breakdown of costs, and the payment arrangements she can offer, including spreading the total over monthly instalments.

The active treatment phase begins once you decide to proceed. For fixed braces, the first step is bonding the brackets and placing the initial archwire. For aligners, you receive your first series of trays along with clear instructions on how to use them. From this point forward, you visit the practice every 6 to 12 weeks. Each appointment is focused and efficient, usually lasting 15 to 30 minutes. she tracks your progress carefully at every visit and adjust the treatment as needed.

After the teeth have reached their final positions, she transitions to the retention phase. This typically involves bonding a thin retainer wire behind the front teeth and providing a removable retainer for overnight wear. she cannots overstate how important this step is: without retention, teeth tend to shift back over time. she monitors retention regularly to make sure your result lasts.
