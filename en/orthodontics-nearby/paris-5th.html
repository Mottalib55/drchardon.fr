The 5th arrondissement and its residents

The 5th arrondissement is the intellectual heart of Paris. The Sorbonne, founded in 1257, has given the entire district its character: the Quartier Latin derives its name from the Latin once spoken by students and scholars in the area. Today, the arrondissement is home to several prestigious institutions, including the École Normale Supérieure, the College de France, the Universite Pierre et Marie Curie (Jussieu) and the Institut du Monde Arabe. This concentration of academic institutions gives the neighbourhood a distinctive intellectual atmosphere.

The Pantheon, dominating the hill of Sainte-Genevieve, is the resting place of France's greatest figures: Voltaire, Rousseau, Hugo, Zola, Curie. Its imposing neoclassical dome is visible from much of the arrondissement and serves as a landmark for all residents. Nearby, the Rue Mouffetard, one of the oldest streets in Paris, offers a lively market atmosphere with its food stalls, cheese shops and wine merchants. This pedestrian-friendly street is beloved by families and is a focal point of neighbourhood life.

The Jardin des Plantes, France's principal botanical garden, occupies 28 hectares along the Seine and includes the Natural History Museum, greenhouses and a small zoo. Families with children frequent this remarkable green space year-round. The Arenes de Lutece, a Gallo-Roman amphitheatre from the 1st century, and the medieval Musee de Cluny with its famous tapestries of The Lady and the Unicorn, add further historical depth to this exceptional arrondissement.
