Orthodontic treatments for patients from Sartrouville

Her practice offers a comprehensive set of orthodontic techniques that she selects based on each patient's clinical needs rather than commercial considerations. For younger children from Sartrouville, interceptive orthodontics is often the starting point. This phase of treatment uses removable appliances, palatal expanders, or partial braces to correct developing skeletal and dental problems -- such as crossbites, severe crowding, or protruding upper incisors -- while the child is still growing. By guiding jaw growth at the right moment, interceptive treatment can reduce the severity of later correction and in some cases avoid the need for extraction of permanent teeth.

For teenagers, multi-bracket braces remain the most versatile and reliable approach. she uses both conventional metal brackets and ceramic (tooth-coloured) brackets depending on the patient's preference and the clinical demands of the case. The brackets are bonded to the teeth and connected by a series of archwires that are progressively adjusted at each appointment. This system allows precise three-dimensional control of tooth position and is particularly effective for significant crowding, rotations, and complex bite corrections.

Adults from Sartrouville increasingly choose clear aligners, a discreet system of custom-made transparent trays that fit over the teeth and are changed every one to two weeks. Aligners are ideal for patients in professional or social settings where the visibility of braces would be a concern. However, they are not suitable for every case -- severe skeletal discrepancies or complex tooth movements may still require brackets. During the initial consultation, she alwayses give an honest evaluation of which technique will produce the best outcome for your specific situation, rather than steering you towards any particular product.

Whatever the technique chosen, the underlying principles are the same: careful planning, controlled force application, and close monitoring throughout treatment. she alsos manage retention after active treatment is complete, fitting bonded wires and providing removable retainers to keep the teeth stable in their new positions.

Treatment process for a patient from Sartrouville

If you contact her practice from Sartrouville, the first step is to schedule an initial consultation. During this appointment, she carries out a complete clinical examination, take digital photographs, and capture a 3D intraoral scan of your teeth -- a comfortable process that takes just a few minutes and replaces the old paste impressions. she alsos prescribe the necessary X-rays (panoramic and cephalometric), which are taken using low-dose digital equipment.

she thens study these records in detail to arrive at a precise diagnosis and draw up a personalised treatment plan. At a second appointment, she walks you through the plan step by step: what the current problems are, what treatment she recommends, how long it is likely to take, and what the costs will be. she alsos explain any alternatives so you can make a fully informed decision. There is never any obligation to proceed on the spot -- she encourages patients to take the plan home, discuss it with family, and come back with questions.

Once treatment begins, you will visit the practice every 6 to 12 weeks for adjustments and progress reviews. Each visit is relatively brief, usually between fifteen and thirty minutes. Between appointments, it is important to maintain excellent oral hygiene (she provides detailed instructions) and to follow any wearing instructions for elastics or aligners. she is available by phone and email between appointments if any issue arises -- a loose bracket, a question about an aligner, or any discomfort that seems unusual.

At the end of active treatment, retention devices are fitted and she schedules follow-up visits at increasing intervals to make sure the result remains stable. The entire journey, from first consultation to the end of active retention monitoring, is managed within her practice, ensuring continuity of care throughout.
