The 18th arrondissement and its residents

The 18th arrondissement is one of the most populated and culturally dynamic districts in Paris, home to roughly 200,000 residents. It is also one of the most diverse, both socially and culturally, encompassing neighbourhoods that range from the tourist-heavy slopes of Montmartre to the lively African and Maghrebi communities of the Goutte d'Or, from the gentrifying streets of Chateau Rouge to the family-oriented calm of the Jules Joffrin quarter.

Montmartre is, of course, the 18th's most famous asset. The Sacre-Coeur basilica, the Place du Tertre, and the narrow, hilly streets around the butte draw millions of visitors each year. But the actual residents of Montmartre tend to live in the quieter streets on the north and west slopes, in an area that retains a genuine village atmosphere. These are often young families, artists, and professionals who value the neighbourhood's character and history.

Beyond Montmartre, the 18th is undergoing significant urban renewal. The Quartier de la Chapelle and the areas around the Gare du Nord are seeing major development projects, attracting a younger demographic of professionals and entrepreneurs. The Rue Ordener market and the Marche de la Chapelle reflect the arrondissement's multicultural identity, with food and goods from across the world.

The population of the 18th includes a notable number of young families, and this is reflected in her patient base from the arrondissement. Parents bring children for early orthodontic assessments, teenagers come for comprehensive treatment, and adults -- many of whom put off treatment during busier life stages -- finally take the step. The 18th's mix of backgrounds also means she sees patients with a wide range of dental anatomies and orthodontic needs, which keeps her clinical practice varied and engaging.

Orthodontic treatments available

Her practice in Colombes provides the full range of orthodontic treatments. she adapts her approach to each patient based on a thorough initial assessment, and she nevers recommend treatment without first establishing a clear diagnosis.

Clear aligners offer a discreet way to correct misaligned teeth. These transparent, removable trays are custom-made from digital scans of your teeth and are worn for a prescribed number of hours each day. They are changed at regular intervals, with each new tray moving the teeth a small step closer to their final position. Aligners are popular with adults and older teenagers who want effective treatment without the visibility of traditional braces. They also make oral hygiene straightforward, since you remove them to brush and floss.

Multi-bracket braces are the workhorse of orthodontic treatment. For cases involving significant crowding, complex bite problems, rotated or impacted teeth, or jaw discrepancies, braces offer unmatched precision and control. she uses contemporary, low-profile brackets that are considerably smaller and more comfortable than older designs. Both metal and ceramic options are available. Ceramic brackets match the tooth colour and are significantly less noticeable.

Interceptive orthodontics is designed for children between approximately 6 and 10 years old. The goal is to address developing problems while the child's jaw is still growing, which can simplify or even eliminate the need for later treatment. Common interventions include palatal expansion, correction of crossbites, and management of habits that affect dental development. she recommends that parents bring their children for an initial orthodontic evaluation around age 7.

Teenage orthodontics is comprehensive treatment carried out once most permanent teeth have erupted, usually between ages 11 and 16. This is the ideal time for full correction, and the choice of technique -- braces or aligners -- depends on the clinical situation and the teenager's willingness to follow the treatment protocol.

Adult orthodontics addresses the needs of patients who may have missed the window for teenage treatment or who have developed new alignment issues over time. Teeth can shift at any age due to changes in jaw structure, tooth loss, or habits such as tongue thrusting. Adult treatment can be purely aesthetic, purely functional, or a combination of both. she sees a growing number of adult patients from the 18th arrondissement who are motivated to improve both the appearance and the health of their smile.

Treatment process

At her practice, every treatment follows a clear, structured process. It begins with an initial consultation during which she carries out a detailed clinical examination. she looks at the alignment and condition of the teeth, the relationship between the upper and lower jaws, the facial profile, and any signs of functional problems. Equally important, she listens to what brought you to the practice and what you hope to achieve.

After the initial consultation, she collects diagnostic records: digital impressions of the teeth, X-rays (panoramic and cephalometric), and a series of photographs. These records are essential for precise treatment planning. she analyses them carefully and develop a tailored treatment proposal, which she presents at a subsequent appointment. This presentation covers the technique, the expected duration, the appointment schedule, and a full breakdown of costs. she encourages patients to take their time with this information and to come back with any questions.

During active treatment, appointments are scheduled every 6 to 12 weeks. Each visit involves adjustments to your appliance or a review of your aligner progress, and she keeps you informed about how things are going. These visits are typically 20 to 30 minutes long. The short travel time from the 18th to Colombes means these appointments can be folded into your regular routine without major disruption.

Once active treatment is finished, she enters the retention phase. she bonds thin wire retainers behind the front teeth and provide removable retainer trays for night wear. Retention is what keeps your teeth in their new positions, and she monitors it during periodic follow-up appointments. she alwayses emphasise to patients that retention is not optional -- it is the final, essential step in achieving lasting results.
