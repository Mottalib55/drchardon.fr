How to reach the practice from Saint-Germain-en-Laye

Saint-Germain-en-Laye sits at the western end of the RER A line, on the heights above the Seine, overlooking the vast Foret de Saint-Germain. By car, the most direct route to my practice in Colombes takes the A13 motorway eastbound, then picks up the A86 heading north before exiting towards Colombes. The total distance is approximately twenty kilometres, and the journey takes around twenty-five minutes outside peak hours. During rush hour, the A13/A86 interchange can add a few minutes, but the overall journey remains manageable. Ample parking is available near the practice on surrounding streets and in a public car park.

By public transport, the RER Line A provides a direct connection from Saint-Germain-en-Laye station to La Defense, a journey of roughly twenty minutes. At La Defense, transfer to the Transilien Line L heading north towards Colombes (Gare du Stade), which takes about ten minutes. Including the walk from the station to the practice, the total door-to-door journey is approximately thirty-five minutes. The RER A runs frequently during peak hours, and the connection at La Defense is well-signposted and straightforward.

For patients coming from the northern side of Saint-Germain-en-Laye, near the Foret or the Chateau, an alternative route by car passes through Le Pecq, Le Vesinet, and Chatou before joining the road to Colombes via Houilles. This route avoids motorway tolls entirely, though it may be slightly longer in terms of time. Whatever your preferred mode of transport, the journey from Saint-Germain-en-Laye to Colombes is uncomplicated and well served by infrastructure.

Saint-Germain-en-Laye and its residents

Saint-Germain-en-Laye is one of the most prestigious communes in the Ile-de-France, with a population of approximately 46,000 and a history that stretches back to the medieval period. The town is dominated by the Chateau de Saint-Germain-en-Laye, a former royal residence that now houses the Musee d'Archeologie Nationale, and by the Grande Terrasse designed by Andre Le Notre, which offers panoramic views over the Seine valley towards Paris. The Foret de Saint-Germain, covering over 3,500 hectares, borders the town to the north and west, providing an extraordinary natural asset for residents.

The town centre is one of the most attractive in the western suburbs, with a pedestrianised Rue de Paris, a lively market on Tuesdays, Fridays, and Sundays, and a wide selection of shops, restaurants, and cultural venues including the Theatre Alexandre Dumas. Saint-Germain-en-Laye is also an important educational centre: the Lycee International de Saint-Germain-en-Laye, with its numerous foreign-language sections, attracts families from across the region and from the expatriate community. The town also has the Lycee Jeanne d'Albret, the College Marcel Roby, and the College Claude Debussy, serving a large population of school-age children and teenagers.

This combination of cultural richness, educational excellence, and quality of life draws families who are accustomed to high standards in every aspect of their lives, including healthcare. Many of my patients from Saint-Germain-en-Laye are parents who want the best possible orthodontic care for their children, professionals who value discretion and expertise, and long-term residents who have come to trust the practice through personal experience. The international character of the town's population is also reflected in my practice -- I am comfortable working with English-speaking patients, and this page exists precisely to serve that community.

Orthodontic treatments for patients from Saint-Germain-en-Laye

My Colombes practice offers the full range of orthodontic treatments, and I match the technique to the patient rather than the other way around. For young children from Saint-Germain-en-Laye, interceptive orthodontics is the appropriate starting point when skeletal or dental problems are identified early. Between the ages of six and ten, I can use appliances such as palatal expanders, functional orthopaedic devices, and partial fixed braces to correct crossbites, narrow arches, protruding incisors, and other developmental issues. Interceptive treatment takes advantage of the child's growth potential and often simplifies -- or in some cases eliminates -- the need for comprehensive treatment later.

Multi-bracket braces are the backbone of treatment for teenagers and for adults with complex orthodontic needs. I offer both metal and ceramic brackets, both of which are far smaller and more comfortable than they were a generation ago. Fixed braces provide the precise, three-dimensional control required for demanding cases: severe crowding, significant rotations, Class II or Class III bite relationships, and asymmetries. Each archwire is selected and adjusted according to the treatment plan, and progress is monitored closely at each appointment.

Clear aligners are very popular among adult patients from Saint-Germain-en-Laye. The transparent, removable trays are designed from a 3D digital scan and changed every one to two weeks, progressively aligning the teeth. Aligners are particularly well-suited to professionals and parents who want a discreet treatment. I plan every aligner case digitally, verifying each stage of movement before production, to maximise predictability. However, I am always transparent about the limits of aligners -- when a case calls for a level of control that aligners cannot reliably provide, I recommend brackets and explain why.

All treatments include retention. I fit bonded retainer wires and provide removable night retainers at the end of active treatment, and I monitor stability through a structured follow-up schedule. Retention is a planned, integral part of every treatment -- it is how I ensure that the result lasts.

Treatment process for a patient from Saint-Germain-en-Laye

Your treatment journey begins with an initial consultation at the Colombes practice. I carry out a comprehensive clinical examination -- teeth, gums, bite, jaw joints, facial symmetry -- and capture a complete set of digital records. These include a 3D intraoral scan (replacing traditional impressions), a panoramic X-ray, a lateral cephalometric X-ray, and clinical photographs. The records provide the raw material for an accurate diagnosis and a well-founded treatment plan.

I then analyse the records carefully, evaluating skeletal proportions, dental crowding, arch form, and soft-tissue relationships. Based on this analysis, I design a treatment plan that I present to you at a dedicated appointment. During this meeting, I display the scans and X-rays, explain the diagnosis clearly, describe the recommended treatment, estimate the timeline, and provide a detailed cost breakdown. I also outline any viable alternatives. My goal is to give you all the information you need to make a confident, informed decision -- and I welcome you to take the plan home to think it over.

During active treatment, you visit the practice every 6 to 12 weeks. Each appointment is purposeful and efficient: I assess the progress of tooth movement, adjust the appliance or deliver new aligner trays, and address any questions. Between visits, I am reachable by phone or email for any concerns -- a detached bracket, discomfort, a question about elastic wear. Treatment duration varies: straightforward aligner cases may take eight to fourteen months, while comprehensive brace treatments typically span eighteen to twenty-four months.

When the teeth have reached their planned positions, active appliances are removed and retention devices are placed. I schedule follow-up appointments at three-month intervals initially, gradually extending to six-monthly and annual reviews. This phase lasts at least two years and is essential for maintaining the stability of the result.
