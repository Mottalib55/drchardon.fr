Poissy and its residents

Poissy is a commune of approximately 40,000 inhabitants with a rich industrial and royal heritage. The Collegiale Notre-Dame de Poissy, a remarkable Romanesque and Gothic church, is one of the oldest in the Ile-de-France and marks the site where King Louis IX (Saint Louis) was baptised in 1214. For much of the twentieth century, Poissy was associated with the automobile industry -- the former Simca and later PSA (now Stellantis) factory has been a major employer and has shaped the town's identity. Though the industrial landscape has evolved, Poissy retains a strong working character alongside its historic and cultural assets.

The town is divided into several distinct neighbourhoods. The centre-ville around the Collegiale and the Place de la Republique is the commercial and administrative heart. The Beauregard neighbourhood to the south-east is a large residential area that has benefited from urban renewal programmes. The riverside areas near the Parc Meissonier, with their views across the Seine towards Carrieres-sous-Poissy, offer a more tranquil environment. Poissy has a well-developed educational network, including the Lycee Le Corbusier, the College Jean Jaures, and the College La Bruyere, serving a substantial school-age population.

This large and socially diverse population generates wide-ranging orthodontic needs. I see children from Poissy whose general dentists have identified early signs of jaw imbalance or crowding, teenagers whose parents want to address overbites or crossbites before they become more difficult to treat, and adults who have decided that now is the time to invest in a straighter smile. The variety of cases keeps my clinical work stimulating and ensures that I maintain broad, up-to-date expertise across all areas of orthodontic practice.

Orthodontic treatments for patients from Poissy

My Colombes practice offers every major orthodontic technique currently in use, and I choose the right tool for each patient based on clinical assessment rather than commercial considerations. For young children from Poissy, interceptive orthodontics targets problems that are best addressed while the child is still growing -- typically between the ages of six and ten. Crossbites, severely crowded arches, open bites, and skeletal growth asymmetries can all be influenced by well-timed intervention using palatal expanders, functional appliances, or partial braces. The goal of interceptive treatment is to redirect growth, create space, and establish a better foundation for the permanent dentition, often reducing the need for extractions or complex corrections later on.

Multi-bracket braces are my primary tool for comprehensive orthodontic treatment in teenagers and adults. I use low-profile brackets in metal or ceramic, bonded directly to the teeth and connected by a series of archwires that I adjust at each visit. This system gives me precise three-dimensional control over tooth position and is the most reliable method for complex cases involving significant crowding, deep bites, open bites, and Class II or Class III skeletal relationships. Ceramic brackets are available for patients who prefer a less visible option, and I discuss the trade-offs -- aesthetics versus robustness -- openly during treatment planning.

Clear aligners have become an important part of my practice, especially for adult patients from Poissy who want a discreet, removable solution. Each aligner series is custom-designed from a 3D digital scan, with every planned tooth movement verified computationally before production. Patients receive a new set of trays every one to two weeks, gradually moving the teeth towards their target positions. Aligners work well for mild to moderate corrections -- spacing, moderate crowding, minor bite adjustments -- but I never recommend them for cases where their limitations would compromise the outcome. When brackets are the better option, I say so clearly.

Retention is an integral part of every treatment I carry out. At the end of active treatment, I fit bonded retainer wires and prescribe removable night retainers to prevent the teeth from drifting back. I then monitor the result through a structured follow-up programme over the following two years.

Treatment process for a patient from Poissy

The treatment process begins with a first consultation at the practice in Colombes. During this appointment, I conduct a thorough clinical examination of the teeth, gums, bite, and jaw joints. I also capture a complete set of digital records: an intraoral 3D scan, a panoramic X-ray, a cephalometric X-ray, and a series of standardised photographs. The 3D scan is fast, comfortable, and far more precise than the old-style putty impressions.

I then devote time to analysing the records, measuring skeletal angles, assessing tooth-size discrepancies, and evaluating arch form. This analysis produces a detailed diagnosis from which I build a personalised treatment plan. At a second appointment, I present the plan to you on screen -- explaining the problems identified, the proposed treatment, the expected timeline, and the costs. I am meticulous about transparency at this stage because I believe that a well-informed patient is a more confident and cooperative partner in treatment. I encourage you to ask questions, and I am happy for you to take the plan home before making a decision.

Active treatment involves regular visits to the practice, spaced every 6 to 12 weeks. Each appointment is used to check progress, make adjustments, and provide updated guidance on home care. Treatment duration depends on the complexity of the case: simpler aligner treatments may take eight to fourteen months, while comprehensive bracket treatments typically span eighteen to twenty-four months. If any issue arises between scheduled visits -- a detached bracket, unexpected discomfort, a question about your aligners -- you can reach me by phone or email and I will arrange a prompt solution.

When active treatment is complete, I remove the braces or confirm the final aligner stage, and place retention devices. Follow-up appointments are scheduled at three-month intervals initially, then every six months, and eventually annually. This structured retention programme ensures that the result we achieved together remains stable over the long term.
