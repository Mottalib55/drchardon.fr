Boulogne-Billancourt and its residents

With approximately 121,000 inhabitants, Boulogne-Billancourt is the largest commune in Ile-de-France outside Paris. This exceptional demographic position reflects the attractiveness of a city that combines quality of life, Parisian proximity and economic dynamism. The history of Boulogne-Billancourt is deeply linked to the cinema and automobile industries: the former Boulogne film studios saw masterpieces of cinema born, while the Ile Seguin long housed the Renault factories. Today, the Ile Seguin hosts an ambitious cultural hub with exhibition spaces.

The Trapeze district, built on former industrial wasteland along the Seine, embodies the city's contemporary transformation. Modern architecture, green spaces, schools and shops have replaced factory warehouses. This neighbourhood has attracted many young families and executives who work at La Defense or in the corporate headquarters located in the city. The Point du Jour, another emblematic district, blends large residential complexes with small houses, while the town centre around Rue d'Aguesseau and Place Marcel Sembat retains the atmosphere of a lively market town.

The population of Boulogne-Billancourt is characterised by a high proportion of executives, professionals and families with children. International schools, proximity to the Bois de Boulogne, and excellent public transport make the city highly sought after by families. This active, educated population is naturally demanding of quality orthodontic care for both children and adults.
