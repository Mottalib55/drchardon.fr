Carrieres-sur-Seine and its residents

Carrieres-sur-Seine is a commune of approximately 16,000 inhabitants that has managed to preserve a distinctly village-like character despite its proximity to Paris. The town occupies a long, narrow strip of land between the hills to the west and the Seine to the east, and this topography gives it a unique setting. The old quarries (carrieres) that gave the town its name are still visible in the hillside, and the area around the Eglise Saint-Jean-Baptiste in the town centre retains much of its historic charm.

The residential areas of Carrieres-sur-Seine are predominantly composed of individual houses and small apartment blocks, attracting families who want space and greenery without giving up access to Paris. The town has several primary schools and a college (College Les Amandiers), and many teenagers attend the lycees in Houilles or Sartrouville. This strong family presence translates into consistent orthodontic need -- parents bring their children for assessment during the mixed-dentition stage, and many of those children go on to full treatment as teenagers.

Carrieres-sur-Seine also benefits from excellent natural surroundings. The Parc de la Borde and the Seine towpath offer pleasant walks, and the town's slightly elevated position provides views over the river valley. This quality of life attracts residents who tend to settle long-term, and she oftens see siblings from the same family over successive years, which is gratifying professionally and speaks to the trust families in Carrieres-sur-Seine place in the practice.

Orthodontic treatments for patients from Carrieres-sur-Seine

The treatments she provides at her Colombes practice cover the full range of orthodontic care. For young children from Carrieres-sur-Seine, she frequentlies perform interceptive orthodontics, addressing developing problems such as posterior crossbites, anterior open bites, or pronounced dental crowding before all the permanent teeth have erupted. Interceptive treatment uses age-appropriate appliances -- palatal expanders, removable plates, or limited brackets on the permanent teeth already present -- and takes advantage of the child's natural growth to guide the jaws into a better relationship. This can shorten the duration and reduce the complexity of any later comprehensive treatment.

Multi-bracket braces are the mainstay of treatment for teenagers, and they remain the most effective technique for cases involving significant crowding, rotated teeth, or complex bite problems. she offers both metal and ceramic brackets. Metal brackets are smaller and more comfortable than they were a decade ago, while ceramic brackets blend with the tooth colour for a less conspicuous appearance. The choice depends on clinical requirements and the patient's personal preference, and she discusses both options at the treatment planning stage.

For adults, clear aligners have become a major part of her practice. These transparent, removable trays are custom-designed from a 3D digital scan and exert gentle, controlled forces to move the teeth progressively. Adults from Carrieres-sur-Seine often appreciate the discretion of aligners, particularly those in client-facing roles. However, she alwayses assess whether aligners are truly the right tool for the case at hand -- some situations still call for brackets, and she will always recommend the approach most likely to produce a stable, high-quality result.

In all cases, she alsos manage the retention phase after active treatment, using bonded retainer wires and removable retainers to prevent relapse. Retention is not an afterthought; it is a planned, integral part of every treatment she carries out.

Treatment process for a patient from Carrieres-sur-Seine

Your journey begins with an initial consultation at the practice in Colombes. During this visit, she performs a thorough clinical examination of the teeth, gums, and jaws, and she captures a complete set of digital records: intraoral 3D scan, panoramic X-ray, cephalometric X-ray, and clinical photographs. These records form the basis of the diagnosis and treatment plan. she thens study them carefully, often spending considerable time analysing the relationships between the teeth, the skeletal structures, and the soft tissues of the face.

At a second appointment, she presents the treatment plan to you in detail. she explains the nature of the problem, the proposed solution, the expected duration, and the estimated cost. she alsos outline any viable alternatives. This transparency is essential -- you should understand exactly what is being proposed and why before you agree to proceed.

Active treatment involves regular visits to the practice, scheduled every 6 to 12 weeks depending on the technique and the stage of treatment. At each visit, she checks the progress of tooth movement, make adjustments to the appliance, and address any concerns you may have. Between appointments, she asks patients to maintain good oral hygiene, avoid hard or sticky foods that could damage braces, and follow any instructions regarding elastic wear or aligner changes.

Once the teeth have reached their target positions, the active appliance is removed and retention devices are placed. she fits a bonded wire behind the lower front teeth (and sometimes the upper), and she provides a removable retainer to wear at night. Follow-up appointments are then scheduled at gradually increasing intervals to verify that the result remains stable. The entire process, from first consultation to retention follow-up, is managed by me personally at the Colombes practice.
