Your dental surgeon in Colombes, near Bois-Colombes

That level of specialisation matters, because orthodontic treatment is not a one-size-fits-all proposition. My practice is equipped with the latest digital imaging tools, including low-dose 3D scanners and intra-oral cameras, so I can plan treatment with a precision that was unimaginable even ten years ago. How to reach the practice from Bois-Colombes

Bois-Colombes shares its southern border directly with Colombes, so the journey between the two towns could hardly be simpler. By car, the most direct route takes you down the Rue des Bourguignons, which runs almost in a straight line from the centre of Bois-Colombes into the heart of Colombes. The entire drive takes about three minutes under normal traffic conditions, and even during the morning rush hour it rarely exceeds five. Street parking is generally available in the area around the practice, and there are several pay-and-display bays within a two-minute walk.

If you prefer public transport, the Transilien L line is your best friend. Bois-Colombes station is served by frequent trains heading towards Saint-Lazare, and the Gare du Stade in Colombes is just one stop away in the opposite direction. The total journey time by public transport, including a short walk at either end, is roughly eight minutes. Several bus routes also link the two towns, including the 304 and the 235, both of which stop within easy reach of the practice. For cyclists, the flat terrain between Bois-Colombes and Colombes makes a bike ride of about five minutes perfectly feasible, and there are dedicated cycle lanes along parts of the route.

Bois-Colombes and its residents

Bois-Colombes is a compact, leafy commune of roughly 29,000 inhabitants nestled between Colombes to the west, Asnieres-sur-Seine to the east, and La Garenne-Colombes to the south. Despite its relatively small surface area of just 1.93 square kilometres, it has a strong identity and a genuine village atmosphere that sets it apart from some of the larger, more anonymous suburbs of the Hauts-de-Seine department. The town centre, clustered around the Rue Andre Joineau and the Place de la Resistance, is full of independent shops, bakeries, and cafes that give it a distinctly unhurried feel.

Families are drawn to Bois-Colombes for its excellent schools, including the well-regarded Groupe Scolaire Gramme and the College Albert Camus, as well as its generous provision of green spaces for a town of its size. The Parc Pierre Lagravere, which straddles the border with Colombes, is a particular favourite for weekend walks. The population is young and dynamic, with a significant proportion of households with children, which means that interceptive and adolescent orthodontics are in strong demand. I see many young patients from Bois-Colombes who are referred by their family dentist for early evaluation, typically around the age of seven, to assess whether the jaw is growing correctly and whether there is enough room for the permanent teeth.

At the same time, a growing number of adults from Bois-Colombes are seeking orthodontic treatment, whether to correct a longstanding issue they never addressed as a teenager or to deal with teeth that have shifted over the years. The proximity of the practice makes it particularly easy for working adults to pop in during a lunch break or on the way home from the station.

Orthodontic treatments for patients from Bois-Colombes

At my practice, I offer the full range of contemporary orthodontic techniques. For children between the ages of six and eleven, interceptive orthodontics can address problems such as crossbites, severe crowding, or protruding upper teeth before the adult dentition is fully in place. These early interventions are often short -- typically six to twelve months -- and can dramatically simplify or even eliminate the need for more extensive treatment later on.

Teenagers from Bois-Colombes most commonly receive multi-bracket braces, which remain the gold standard for complex tooth movements. Modern brackets are smaller, more comfortable, and more efficient than those of a generation ago, and I use self-ligating systems that reduce friction and can shorten overall treatment time. For teens who are concerned about the appearance of traditional braces, I also offer ceramic brackets that blend with the colour of the teeth, making them far less noticeable.

Adults often gravitate towards clear aligners, a series of custom-made, virtually invisible trays that gradually shift the teeth into their desired positions. These are removable, which means there are no dietary restrictions and oral hygiene remains straightforward. I use advanced digital planning software to design each aligner sequence, and patients can see a simulation of their expected results before treatment even begins. However, aligners are not suitable for every clinical situation, and part of my role is to provide honest, clear guidance about which technique will deliver the best outcome for each individual case.

Regardless of the technique chosen, every treatment plan I create is tailored to the individual. No two mouths are alike, and the goal is always the same: teeth that function well, look natural, and remain stable for the long term.

Treatment process for a patient from Bois-Colombes

The first step is a thorough initial consultation at the practice. During this appointment, I carry out a clinical examination of the teeth, the gums, and the jaws, and we discuss what you would like to achieve. If I believe orthodontic treatment is appropriate, we then move on to a records appointment, where I take digital photographs, X-rays, and a three-dimensional scan of your teeth. These records allow me to formulate a precise diagnosis and to design a treatment plan that addresses your specific needs.

Once the plan is ready, I present it to you in detail, explaining the proposed mechanics, the estimated duration, and the expected cost. I believe strongly in transparency: you should understand exactly what is going to happen, why, and how long it will take before you commit. For patients from Bois-Colombes, the fitting appointment -- whether that means bonding brackets or delivering the first set of aligners -- is usually the next step.

Active treatment typically lasts between twelve and twenty-four months, depending on the complexity of the case. Throughout this period, you will visit the practice every 6 to 12 weeks for adjustments or to collect your next set of aligners. Given that Bois-Colombes is only three minutes away, these appointments slot easily into even the busiest schedule. At the end of active treatment, a retention phase begins: this usually involves a thin wire bonded to the back of the front teeth and, in some cases, a removable retainer worn at night. Retention is critical to ensuring that the results last, and I monitor all my patients carefully during this phase.
