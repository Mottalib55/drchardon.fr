The 3rd arrondissement and its residents

The 3rd arrondissement of Paris is one of the oldest in the capital. Located on the Right Bank of the Seine, it occupies the northern part of the historic Marais district. With approximately 34,000 inhabitants, it is a human-scale arrondissement with remarkable cultural density. It extends primarily around the Quartier du Temple, the Quartier des Arts-et-Metiers and the Haut Marais, a district that has become one of the most sought-after in Paris for its blend of historic elegance and contemporary creativity.

The heritage of the 3rd is exceptional. The Musee Picasso, housed in the Hotel Sale, presents one of the world's largest collections dedicated to the artist. The Musee Carnavalet, recently renovated, traces the history of Paris from prehistory to the present day through fascinating collections. The Archives Nationales, housed in the Hotel de Soubise and the Hotel de Rohan, constitute a unique documentary treasure. The Conservatoire national des arts et metiers and its museum complete this first-rate cultural offering.

The streets of the 3rd arrondissement reflect an authentic neighbourhood life. The Rue de Bretagne, with its Marche des Enfants Rouges (the oldest covered market in Paris, founded in 1615), is the beating heart of the arrondissement. Independent boutiques, art galleries, design studios and speciality coffee shops coexist alongside stone buildings from the 17th and 18th centuries. The Square du Temple, former enclosure of the Knights Templar, provides a green space appreciated by families and walkers.

The population of the 3rd is diverse and dynamic. It includes families with children attending local schools, young professionals attracted by the cultural vibrancy of the Marais, liberal professionals and artists based in converted former workshops. This diversity is reflected in her patient base: she treats children requiring interceptive orthodontics as well as adults seeking discreet aligner treatment, and teenagers needing multi-bracket braces. Residents of the 3rd are often demanding in terms of care and appreciate a practitioner who takes the time to explain, reassure and personalise each treatment.

Orthodontic treatments offered to patients from the 3rd

Her practice is exclusively dedicated to orthodontics, which allows her to offer in-depth expertise across all available techniques. Clear aligners are the most requested treatment by patients from the 3rd arrondissement. This system relies on a series of transparent custom-made trays created from digital impressions of the teeth. Each tray is worn for approximately two weeks before being replaced by the next, progressively moving the teeth towards their ideal position. Aligners are virtually invisible once in the mouth, making them the preferred solution for adults and teenagers concerned about discretion. They are removable, which simplifies meals, brushing and social occasions.

Multi-bracket braces remain the reference technique for complex orthodontic cases. Dr Chardon offers ceramic brackets, whose colour blends with that of natural tooth enamel, and metal brackets, more resistant and indicated in demanding clinical situations. Braces allow very precise three-dimensional control of tooth movements. In teenagers, this is often the technique of choice, as it accompanies growth and effectively treats severe malocclusions, jaw discrepancies and bite problems.

Interceptive orthodontics concerns children from the age of 6-7. An early assessment can detect developmental abnormalities: a palate that is too narrow, persistent thumb-sucking, mouth breathing, insufficient space for permanent teeth, or a discrepancy between the jaws. A short treatment at this age, generally 6 to 12 months, can correct these problems before they worsen and avoid more extensive treatment during adolescence. Dr Chardon strongly recommends that parents in the 3rd arrondissement consult early, even in the absence of an apparent problem, to benefit from a specialist opinion. The French Society of Orthodontics and the American Association of Orthodontists agree on the importance of this first assessment before the age of 7.

Adult orthodontics is a growing field. An increasing number of patients in their thirties, forties and beyond come to the practice to correct alignment issues they have lived with for years, or to prepare for prosthetic or implant work in collaboration with their regular dentist. Adult treatment requires a specific approach, taking into account periodontal status, any existing restorations and the patient's aesthetic expectations. Dr Chardon adapts to each situation to propose the most relevant plan that is respectful of the patient's dental health.

Treatment process for a patient from the 3rd arrondissement

The orthodontic pathway at the practice follows a rigorous protocol, designed to ensure the quality of the result while respecting each patient's comfort and time. Everything begins with a thorough initial consultation. Dr Chardon welcomes you at the Colombes practice for a complete examination: clinical analysis of your teeth, intraoral and extraoral photographs, panoramic X-ray and, if necessary, a lateral cephalometric radiograph. She listens carefully to your expectations and concerns, because a successful orthodontic treatment relies on mutual understanding between practitioner and patient.

Based on this assessment, she establishes a precise diagnosis and designs a treatment plan that she presents to you in complete transparency. You know the techniques being considered, the estimated duration, the expected results and the overall budget before making your decision. She systematically proposes several alternatives when the clinical situation permits, so that you can choose the option that best suits your lifestyle and constraints. For patients from the 3rd, she takes the travel time into account and organises sessions to limit the number of trips as much as possible.

Follow-up appointments are scheduled every 6 to 12 weeks depending on the type of appliance. Each session is organised to minimise waiting time. Dr Chardon checks the progress of treatment, makes necessary adjustments (arc activation, tray changes, bracket bonding or repositioning) and verifies oral hygiene. Between appointments, she remains contactable by phone or email for any urgent questions, a detached bracket or unusual discomfort. This availability is essential so that you feel supported throughout the process.

The retention phase marks the end of active treatment, but not the end of follow-up. This is a step she takes very seriously, as it guarantees the long-term stability of results. A retention wire is bonded behind the teeth (invisible from the outside) and/or a night retainer tray is made. She schedules retention checks for at least one year, then annual visits to verify that the result is maintained over time. A beautiful smile should last: that is the promise she makes to each of her patients.
